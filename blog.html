A Beginner's Guide to the Gym

Posted October 15, 2026

Walking into a gym for the first time is intimidating. Everyone seems to know exactly what they're doing, and you don't know where to start. You don't need a complicated program. Here's the simple plan I'd give anyone who's just starting out:

  1. Go three days a week. Full-body workouts on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday give you plenty of practice and enough time to recover.
  2. Build around the big compound lifts: squat, bench press, deadlift, overhead press, and rows. Each one works several muscles at once, so you get more out of every session.
  3. Learn good form before you add weight. Start light, watch a few tutorials, and film yourself if you're not sure a rep looks right.
  4. Add a little weight or one more rep each week. This is called progressive overload, and it's how you keep getting stronger instead of stalling.
  5. Write down every workout. A notes app is fine. If you don't track your lifts, you can't tell whether you're improving.
  6. Eat enough protein and sleep seven to nine hours. Your muscles actually grow while you recover, not while you lift.

Most of all, keep showing up. The best program is the one you'll actually stick with. A decent plan you follow for a year beats a perfect plan you quit after two weeks.

How I Think About Investing: Buy the S&P 500

Posted October 1, 2026

I'm not a financial advisor, and this isn't financial advice. But my approach to investing is boring on purpose, and it comes down to three ideas.

Buy the S&P 500

The S&P 500 tracks about 500 of the largest companies in the U.S. One low-cost index fund gets you a small piece of all of them, so you don't have to guess which company will win. Over the long run it has averaged roughly 10% a year, even though some years it drops a lot.

Start early

Time matters more than how much you put in. Because of compound interest, your returns start earning returns of their own. Money invested in your twenties has decades longer to grow than money invested in your thirties, so even small amounts add up. The hard part isn't picking investments. It's not touching them when the market drops.

Spend wisely

You can only invest money you haven't already spent. Spending wisely doesn't mean never buying anything fun (festival tickets aren't cheap). It means knowing where your money goes and choosing on purpose, so there's always something left over to invest.
